# Norris finds pole as the rain arrives, and takes the last word off Russell

By the time qualifying began at Zandvoort, [George Russell](/drivers/george-russell) had beaten [Lando Norris](/drivers/lando-norris) twice in two days: by 0.041s for sprint pole on Friday, then by 1.360s in the sprint itself on Saturday morning. Norris got the last word, and he got it in the hardest possible conditions.

Rain was falling over the closing minutes. Grip was going away rather than coming to them. Norris produced a 1:11.163 and beat Russell by 0.102s.

"I was pushing because every one-hundredth can make a big difference in the end," he said afterwards, which on this evidence was not a platitude.

The top four were separated by 0.142s. On Friday the top four in sprint qualifying were separated by 0.099s. Two sessions, two days, roughly a tenth covering four cars each time.

## Piastri led on pace and lost on timing

[Oscar Piastri](/drivers/oscar-piastri) was the quickest man in Q1 on a 1:12.610 and the second quickest in Q2 on a 1:11.641. He qualified fourth. That is a session where the pace was there for most of an hour and gone for the two minutes that counted.

## Mercedes on the second row of the fight, Ferrari nowhere near it

[Kimi Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) recovered from a Friday he would rather forget, when he put a wheel in the gravel in SQ1 and carried floor damage to fifth, and out-qualified both Ferraris. Third, 0.133s off pole.

[Lewis Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) was fifth and [Charles Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc) sixth, and that is the puzzle of Ferrari's weekend. Leclerc had finished the sprint second, having taken the place off Norris with a genuine overtake at Turn 1. Against the clock he was four tenths off. Quick in race trim, ordinary on a single lap.

## The number Verstappen will carry out of Zandvoort

Seventh, 0.455s off pole. And 0.115s ahead of [Liam Lawson](/drivers/liam-lawson), his stand-in team-mate, who was eighth on a 1:11.733.

[Max Verstappen](/drivers/max-verstappen) has three wins here and the circuit's modern history is essentially his. Its last qualifying session put him on the fourth row, within a tenth of a driver drafted in at short notice.

At the back, [Aston Martin](/teams/aston-martin) put both cars out in Q1 with new Honda hardware, and both [Cadillacs](/teams/cadillac) filled the last row.

## Sprint result against qualifying result

The two Saturday sessions disagreed with each other in an interesting way.

| Driver | Sprint | Qualifying |
|---|---|---|
| Russell | 1st | 2nd |
| Leclerc | 2nd | 6th |
| Norris | 3rd | 1st |
| Antonelli | 4th | 3rd |
| Piastri | 5th | 4th |
| Verstappen | 6th | 7th |
| Hamilton | 7th | 5th |

Leclerc is the outlier by four places. If Ferrari's race pace on Saturday morning was real, he starts the Grand Prix from further back than he deserves, on a circuit that historically does not let you fix that.

## Race day

Paddock's own forecast for Zandvoort has the 15:00 hour at 19C with a 20% chance of rain, drying through the afternoon: 16% at 16:00 and 14% by 17:00. The reverse of Saturday, which ended with rain at the sprint flag and a wet threat hanging over qualifying.

A dry race on a circuit where pole has been decisive, at the end of the last weekend Formula 1 will ever spend here.
