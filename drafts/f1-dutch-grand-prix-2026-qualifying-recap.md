# Norris takes pole as the rain arrives, and gets the last word off Russell

By the time qualifying began, [George Russell](/drivers/george-russell) had beaten [Lando Norris](/drivers/lando-norris) twice in two days at a circuit neither of them will visit again. Four hundredths for sprint pole on Friday afternoon. Then 1.360s in the sprint itself on Saturday morning, from the front, in control. Norris got the last word, and he got it in the one set of conditions where getting it is hardest.

Rain was falling through the closing minutes. Grip was leaving the track rather than arriving on it, which is the version of a late qualifying run where the fast lap is the one you have already done, not the one you are about to attempt. Norris went out anyway and produced a [**1:11.163**](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2026/races/1292/netherlands/qualifying), and Russell's answer was 0.102s short. "I was pushing because every one-hundredth can make a big difference in the end," Norris [told Formula 1](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/norris-denies-russell-pole-position-in-gripping-conclusion-to-qualifying-for-dutch-grand-prix.2DoHZDC14R5tY7ygGaHCz) afterwards, which on this occasion was a statement of method rather than a platitude. It is his second pole of the season.

[Kimi Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) was third at +0.133 and [Oscar Piastri](/drivers/oscar-piastri) fourth at +0.142. So the top four were inside 0.142s, having been [inside 0.099s in sprint qualifying](https://www.the-race.com/formula-1/dutch-gp-f1-sprint-qualifying-2026-russell-pole/) the day before. Two sessions, two days, four cars each time separated by roughly a tenth of a second, at the circuit everyone complains produces nothing.

Piastri's hour is the one worth staring at. He was the fastest man in the first phase on a 1:12.610 and second fastest in the second on a 1:11.641, and he qualified fourth. That is a driver who had the pace for fifty-eight minutes of an hour and did not have it for the two that counted, on a weekend where he has also been out-qualified and out-raced by his team-mate. [McLaren](/teams/mclaren) get the pole; the reigning champion is not the one having the smoother weekend of the two.

Antonelli, for his part, recovered a Friday he will want to forget. He put a wheel in the gravel in the first phase of sprint qualifying, carried floor damage through to fifth, then came back on Saturday and out-qualified both Ferraris. Third on the grid, 0.133s off pole, at the circuit where he collected two penalty points last year for putting Leclerc into a barrier. He is repaying that debt in instalments.

## Ferrari's weekend, in two contradictory numbers

[Charles Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc) finished the sprint second, having taken the place off Norris with a real overtake at Turn 1 on lap 18. A few hours later he qualified sixth, four tenths off pole, behind [Lewis Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) in fifth.

That is the puzzle of [Ferrari](/teams/ferrari) at Zandvoort and arguably of Ferrari in 2026. Quick when the race is long and the tyres matter, ordinary when it comes down to one lap on low fuel. If their sprint pace was the real thing, Leclerc starts Sunday from further back than the car deserves, at a circuit where every winner since it returned in 2021 has started from pole. That is not a small problem. It is the whole problem.

## Seventh, and within a tenth of the stand-in

[Max Verstappen](/drivers/max-verstappen) qualified seventh, 0.455s off pole, at the last Dutch Grand Prix Zandvoort will ever hold. Three wins here, all three from pole, and never a win from anywhere else. The fourth row is not where that record gets extended.

The number that will sting more sits directly behind him. [Liam Lawson](/drivers/liam-lawson) was eighth on a 1:11.733, which is **0.115s** off Verstappen, in a car he had never driven before Friday, on a weekend containing one hour of practice, having been called up at short notice because [Isack Hadjar](/drivers/isack-hadjar) is injured, the Frenchman having [reportedly fractured his wrist](https://www.planetf1.com/news/isack-hadjar-wrist-injury-fresh-details-dutch-gp) in the gym over the break. Read that as praise for Lawson or as a question about [Red Bull](/teams/red-bull-racing)'s season; either reading is available and neither is comfortable.

At the back, [Aston Martin](/teams/aston-martin) put both cars out in the first phase with their upgraded Honda hardware, [Fernando Alonso](/drivers/fernando-alonso) eighteenth and [Lance Stroll](/drivers/lance-stroll) nineteenth, and both [Cadillacs](/teams/cadillac) filled the last row. [Carlos Sainz](/drivers/carlos-sainz) went out in the first phase too, seventeenth, closing a weekend that began with three trips into the gravel and a [pit-lane start in the sprint](https://www.gpblog.com/en/breaking-news/pit-lane-start-for-sainz-as-williams-breaks-parc-ferme-to-reset-difficult-dutch-gp-weekend) after Williams broke parc fermé to fix the damage.

## The grid, for real this time

No penalties reordered anybody. [The starting grid](https://racingnews365.com/full-starting-grid-for-2026-f1-dutch-grand-prix-at-zandvoort) reads exactly as qualifying finished, from Norris on pole to [Sergio Pérez](/drivers/sergio-perez) twenty-second, with one change: Pérez starts from the pit lane after a rule breach. He was last anyway, so it costs him little beyond the indignity.

Which means the front two rows are McLaren, Mercedes, Mercedes, McLaren, in that order. Mercedes had won eight of the eleven races before this weekend and took the first ten poles of the season; the front row here belongs to the team that ended that run in Hungary. [GPFans has the grid](https://www.gpfans.com/en/f1-news/1089376/f1-dutch-grand-prix-starting-grid-2026-all-penalties-applied/) with every penalty applied, for anyone who wants to check it against the timing sheet.

## Race day

Paddock's own hour-by-hour forecast for the circuit has the 15:00 start at 19C with a 20% chance of rain, easing to 16% at 16:00 and 14% by 17:00. Dry, and drying further, which is the exact reverse of Saturday: rain on the sprint's chequered flag, a wet threat over qualifying, and then a clean Sunday.

That matters more here than at most places. Water flattens a car advantage and hands a race to whoever gambles best. A dry track at Zandvoort hands it to whoever starts at the front, and since 2021 the man who starts at the front has won all five times.

**For the books:** the last qualifying session in Zandvoort's Formula 1 history, Norris' second pole of 2026, a fourth consecutive session this weekend decided inside two tenths, and a stand-in Red Bull driver within 0.115s of a four-time world champion at that driver's home race.

The full order is on the [qualifying page](/series/f1/weekend/12/qualifying), Saturday morning's race is on the [sprint page](/series/f1/weekend/12/sprint), the [teams' reactions to both sessions](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/what-the-teams-said-sprint-day-and-qualifying-in-the-netherlands-2026.1vJ9dLmIstL3qivbyY0OEu) are worth a read, and the [Dutch Grand Prix hub](/series/f1/weekend/12) carries session times in your own timezone. Season totals sit on the [standings](/series/f1/standings).

Pole has won every Dutch Grand Prix since the circuit came back. On the last afternoon it will ever get, pole belongs to a McLaren, the crowd came for a Red Bull on the fourth row, and the forecast has taken away the one thing that could have scrambled it.
