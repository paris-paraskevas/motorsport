# Sachsenring, the title filter: Martín leads by seven as Márquez hunts a tenth

MotoGP reaches the halfway point of its 2026 season at the Sachsenring this weekend, and it does so as a genuine four-way fight. **Just 40 points cover the top five riders, and only 25 separate the top four** — and for the first time all season, it is Jorge Martín who leads them. Germany is the last race before the summer break, on a circuit that has answered to one man for the best part of a decade. The paddock has a name for the weekend: a title filter.

## Martín in front — for now

Martín arrives on 193 points, seven clear of his Aprilia team-mate Marco Bezzecchi, having taken the championship lead for the first time this year at Assen — where Bezzecchi crashed out of the lead for a third straight weekend without a score. Behind them the pressure never lets up: Fabio Di Giannantonio (177) and breakout rookie Ai Ogura (168) are both still inside 25 points of top spot. It is the tightest the table has been all season, and a weekend of two races — Saturday's Sprint and Sunday's Grand Prix — can rewrite it entirely before the break. The current table is on [the MotoGP standings tab](/series/motogp/standings).

## Márquez and the fortress

No Sachsenring preview can look past Marc Márquez. The reigning champion has won here nine times in the premier class — every year from 2013 to 2019, again in 2021, and last season — and the circuit's near-endless left-handers have long made him the favourite before a wheel turns. Yet he comes to Germany only fifth, 40 points down, chasing a tenth Sachsenring victory that would haul him back into a fight he cannot yet lead. He has also spoken candidly this season about the mental toll of a seventh operation on his arm. If anyone can bend this title filter, it is the man who owns the place.

## Ogura, chasing history

Ogura turns up fresh from a maiden MotoGP win at Assen — the first for a Japanese rider since Makoto Tamada in 2004. No rider from Japan has led the premier-class standings in 51 years, and none has ever won back-to-back premier-class races. Ogura, remarkably, is within range of both.

## A depleted grid

The entry list tells its own story before the lights go out. Johann Zarco misses the round after a heavy Barcelona crash left him with cruciate-ligament damage, a torn meniscus and a fractured fibula; 40-year-old Cal Crutchlow — a three-time Sachsenring podium finisher — stands in again at LCR Honda. Maverick Viñales is still short of strength in a troublesome right shoulder. The Sachsenring rewards the fully fit, and several on this grid are anything but.

## The circuit

At 3.671 km the Sachsenring is one of the shortest laps of the year, and among the most singular: roughly 80% of it is spent on the side of the tyre, almost all of it turning left. Its signature is the 'Waterfall' — a 21-metre drop into the long left-hander that feeds the back straight, taken almost blind at close to 180 mph. Outright engine power counts for little here; corner speed, front-end confidence and tyre management decide it.

## What's at stake

With big points on offer in both the Sprint and the Grand Prix, and a title fight this compressed, every session before the shutdown matters — momentum banked at the Sachsenring has to last a month. Session times in your local zone, the full schedule and results as they land are all on [the German Grand Prix weekend page](/series/motogp/weekend/11).

The lights go out for the Grand Prix on Sunday; after that, MotoGP scatters for the summer — and whoever leads Germany out gets to enjoy the break the most.
