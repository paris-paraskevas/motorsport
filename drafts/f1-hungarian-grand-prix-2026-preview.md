# Ferrari fancy it, Hamilton owns it, Antonelli leads it: welcome to Monaco without the walls

The [Hungarian Grand Prix](/series/f1/weekend/11) is the eleventh of twenty-two rounds, which makes Sunday the exact halfway point of the season and the last thing anyone does before disappearing for three weeks. [Kimi Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) turns up 45 points clear, six wins from ten, and the youngest championship leader the sport has ever had. He would quite like to spend August sitting on a lead that reads like a verdict. The Hungaroring, inconveniently for him, is the one track on the calendar built to make a Mercedes look ordinary and a Ferrari look reborn.

## Half-term report

[Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) leads on 204, and the genuinely interesting fight is the one behind him. Three drivers are squabbling over second: [Lewis Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) on 159, [George Russell](/drivers/george-russell) on 154 and [Charles Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc) on 126, covered by 33 points, two of whom spent the first lap of [Belgium](/series/f1/weekend/10) trading paint and blame before Hamilton collected a penalty and Russell collected a gravel trap. The [standings](/series/f1/standings) describe a title race that is really a procession with an excellent undercard.

## Monaco without the walls

They call the Hungaroring "Monaco without the walls", and they are not being generous. Fourteen corners, one actual straight, and overtaking that has always run from difficult to strictly theoretical. Whether that survives 2026 is the open question: this is the first time the new cars, with their active aero and an override boost where DRS used to be, have seen the place, and nobody yet knows whether they crack it open or leave it as sealed as ever. Good. Keep everyone guessing. The rest has not changed: a high-downforce, low-patience track where the corners spill into one another and a single mistake ruins the next three, where keeping the brakes cool is harder than stopping the car, and where Saturday afternoon probably still decides Sunday. A one-stop usually wins. Track position is usually everything, though "usually" is carrying more weight this year than it used to. It is also where [Esteban Ocon](/drivers/esteban-ocon) won his only race in 2021, purely because everyone ahead of him crashed, so the place is not above the occasional act of God.

## Ferrari's to fancy

Which brings us to [Ferrari](/teams/ferrari) and the annual delivery of hopium. The SF-26 has spent the year a few kph shy of Toto's rocketships in a straight line, but it has been one of the best cars in the sport through the corners, and the Hungaroring offers precisely nowhere to hide the difference. They have reportedly bolted even more downforce onto it for the occasion. [Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc) has taken pole here in each of the last two years and won on neither, which is either a warning or a tradition. [Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) has won at this circuit a record eight times and treats it like a second home, a claim only slightly undermined by his twelfth place here last summer. If Ferrari cannot win this weekend, they should probably stop promising that they can.

## The corner myth

None of which means [Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) is in any bother, and here is the bit the Ferrari optimists would rather skip. On a track with no straights, [Mercedes](/teams/mercedes)' one flaw, straight-line speed, stops mattering at all. But the real tell came in Belgium's second sector, the twisty middle where the lap is made in the corners rather than on the power: Antonelli finally found his front end and went within a few thousandths of [Verstappen](/drivers/max-verstappen) through it. The Hungaroring is essentially one long second sector. A car that can shadow a Red Bull through the bends at Spa is not a car that turns up to Budapest afraid. He is the bookmakers' favourite, he is the form driver, and this was meant to be the weekend that finally exposed the Mercedes. It may not lay a finger on it.

## McLaren's title defence, now with homework

Spare a thought for the reigning champion. [Lando Norris](/drivers/lando-norris) is fifth, 101 points behind a lead he is supposed to be defending, and yet the Hungaroring ought to be [McLaren](/teams/mclaren)'s garden: they have won the last two here, Norris in 2025 and [Oscar Piastri](/drivers/oscar-piastri) in 2024. The catch is that this year's car has a low-speed-corner problem, which is exactly the wrong flaw to bring to a track made almost entirely of slow and medium corners. Hence the upgrades: a new floor, plus a rotating "Macarena" rear wing they intend to try on Friday and then, apparently, not race. It is the last chance to learn what the thing actually is before summer swallows three weeks.

## The gamblers' last homework

Because everyone treats Hungary as the final assignment before the holidays, the upgrade trucks arrived full. [Aston Martin](/teams/aston-martin), second from bottom with a single point to [Fernando Alonso](/drivers/fernando-alonso)'s name, bring an Adrian Newey-designed B-spec so extensive it needed a fresh crash test to be legal, the biggest single roll of the dice of their season. [Red Bull](/teams/red-bull-racing) bring back the wing they abandoned at Spa, on a track that will finally tell them whether it works. Everyone wants a good number to sit on for three weeks.

## Where it leaves us

So: a runaway leader on the one track that ought to test his car, a Ferrari that has run out of excuses, a champion trying to remember how it is done, and a midfield flinging parts at the wall before the beach. Overtaking has always been hard, though the new cars might yet have a say, so watch qualifying regardless. A one-stop usually wins, so watch the strategy. Session times, live and in your own timezone, are on the [weekend page](/series/f1/weekend/11), and the race is on F1 TV on Sunday. After that, nothing until Zandvoort on 23 August. This is the last flag before F1 goes quiet, and Antonelli would love a comfortable one. The Hungaroring rarely obliges.
