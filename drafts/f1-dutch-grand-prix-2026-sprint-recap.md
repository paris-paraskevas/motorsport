# Russell wins Zandvoort's only sprint, and the rain waits for the flag

[George Russell](/drivers/george-russell) started the first sprint race Zandvoort has ever held from pole and won the only one it will ever hold from the front. Twenty-four laps, [30:25.318](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2026/races/1292/netherlands/sprint-results), 1.360s in hand, and a shower that had been hanging over the dunes all morning arriving at the exact moment he crossed the line. If you were scripting a farewell you would probably not dare.

He got the launch right, led into Turn 1, and had 1.6s by lap 10. The lead moved around a little after that without ever looking like it was in danger. It is the third time he has turned a sprint pole into a sprint win, and given he took that pole having [topped neither qualifying segment](https://www.the-race.com/formula-1/dutch-gp-f1-sprint-qualifying-2026-russell-pole/) on Friday, the whole weekend so far has been Russell finding speed exactly when the timing screens are live.

## Leclerc went and took it

The race behind him was better. [Lando Norris](/drivers/lando-norris) held second for seventeen laps with [Charles Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc) sitting inside a couple of tenths of the McLaren for most of them, close enough to be a nuisance and never quite close enough to do anything. On lap 18 he stopped waiting, put the [Ferrari](/teams/ferrari) down the inside at Turn 1 and made it stick. Friday's sprint qualifying had [put those two within 0.055s of each other](https://www.motorsport.com/f1/news/f1-dutch-gp-sprint-qualifying-report/10847946/), so the pass had been coming since the timing screens went live the day before.

At a circuit whose entire reputation is that overtaking does not happen there, that is worth more than one position. Leclerc had also started on softs, one of only two drivers who did, everybody else having gone for mediums. Ferrari looked at one hour of practice, made a call nobody else made, and finished second with it. On a weekend where their qualifying pace kept letting them down, their race thinking did not.

Norris held third at +5.196 and [Kimi Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) could not find a way past him, coming home fourth at +5.581 after taking [Oscar Piastri](/drivers/oscar-piastri) at Turn 2 on the opening lap. Piastri was fifth, [Max Verstappen](/drivers/max-verstappen) sixth at +10.529 with [Lewis Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) briefly all over the back of him, and Hamilton seventh.

## The last point, and then a cliff

[Pierre Gasly](/drivers/pierre-gasly) took the eighth and final point for [Alpine](/teams/alpine) at +42.510, which tells you how quickly this field spread out once the top seven had settled. [Gabriel Bortoleto](/drivers/gabriel-bortoleto) was ninth at +44.437 and under investigation for an unsafe release, [Arvid Lindblad](/drivers/arvid-lindblad) tenth, and [Liam Lawson](/drivers/liam-lawson) eleventh in the Red Bull he had never driven before Friday.

Three cars had their weekend decided before the lights. [Carlos Sainz](/drivers/carlos-sainz) and [Fernando Alonso](/drivers/fernando-alonso) both started from the pit lane, and not for the ordinary reason: both [Williams](/teams/williams) and [Aston Martin](/teams/aston-martin) changed setup under parc fermé without the Technical Delegate's approval, which carries a mandatory sanction. [Williams did it](https://www.gpblog.com/en/breaking-news/pit-lane-start-for-sainz-as-williams-breaks-parc-ferme-to-reset-difficult-dutch-gp-weekend) because Sainz's three trips into the gravel on Friday had damaged his floor badly enough that the team wanted the suspension rethought overnight; [Aston Martin did the same thing](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/sainz-alonso-start-pit-lane-094020064.html) with a car that had already qualified last. [Sergio Pérez](/drivers/sergio-perez)'s [Cadillac](/teams/cadillac) needed repairs in the pit lane before the formation lap had finished and he came home three laps down.

And [Nico Hulkenberg](/drivers/nico-hulkenberg) lasted seven laps. A power unit failure ended the [Audi](/teams/audi) that had been the quiet story of Friday practice, seventh fastest on the medium compound while everyone ahead used softs. The one session where Audi looked genuinely well prepared was followed by the one result that made it irrelevant.

## What it did to the fight behind Antonelli

Nothing changed at the top and everything tightened underneath it. Antonelli still leads, and he spent the sprint in fourth watching two of the three men chasing him score more than he did.

Russell took the maximum eight and moved to 168. Hamilton took two and sits on 171. **Three points** now separate them in the fight for second in the championship, and they are not team-mates, which is the part that will make the rest of the season interesting. Leclerc's seven took him to 145 and Norris' six to 134, so the four drivers behind Antonelli are covered by 37 points with twelve rounds left. Antonelli's own lead is 53 over Hamilton and 56 over Russell: still comfortable, no longer serene.

Worth saying plainly, because the arithmetic is the story: [Mercedes](/teams/mercedes) have the championship leader and the man closing fastest on second place, and they are in the same garage. Zandvoort's sprint was the first weekend this season where that has looked like a question rather than a luxury. The [teams' own verdicts](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/what-the-teams-said-sprint-day-and-qualifying-in-the-netherlands-2026.1vJ9dLmIstL3qivbyY0OEu) on the day are worth reading side by side.

Russell's own read afterwards was not triumphant. "Obviously a great start to the weekend. The Ferraris look fast so it's gonna be tight this afternoon," he told [Formula 1](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/russell-surges-to-victory-in-zandvoort-sprint-ahead-of-leclerc-and-norris.3evWfVZ0yONnfGGp3t8qyK). He was right that it would be tight and wrong about who from. Ferrari were nowhere near pole a few hours later. Norris was.

## Where it leaves us

**For the books:** the first and last sprint race in Zandvoort's history, Russell's third sprint win, his third sprint pole converted, Leclerc's soft-tyre gamble paying off with the only real overtake of the afternoon, and rain arriving on the chequered flag rather than a lap before it.

The full order is on the [sprint page](/series/f1/weekend/12/sprint), the running totals on the [standings](/series/f1/standings), and the rest of the weekend on the [Dutch Grand Prix hub](/series/f1/weekend/12). The [grid it produced](https://www.crash.net/f1/news/1102700/1/f1-2026-dutch-grand-prix-full-starting-grid-zandvoort-sprint-race) is on Crash.

Zandvoort held one sprint race in its life, gave it a four-hundredths pole, an overtake at a corner where overtakes are not supposed to happen, and rain timed to the second. For a circuit accused of being boring, it is going out with a certain amount of nerve.
