# Russell wins Zandvoort's only sprint, and the rain waits for the flag

[George Russell](/drivers/george-russell) started the first sprint race Zandvoort has ever held from pole, and finished the only one it will ever hold in front. Twenty-four laps, a 1.360s margin over [Charles Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc), and a shower that had been threatening all afternoon arriving at the precise moment he crossed the line.

He got the launch right, led into Turn 1, and by lap 10 had stretched it to 1.6s. It is the third time he has turned a sprint pole into a sprint win.

Look at the gap between eighth and ninth: 42.510s to 44.437s. The last point of the sprint went to [Pierre Gasly](/drivers/pierre-gasly) by under two seconds, forty seconds behind the winner, which tells you how quickly this field spread out once the front four had sorted themselves.

## The move of the afternoon

[Lando Norris](/drivers/lando-norris) held second for seventeen laps with Leclerc inside a couple of tenths of him for most of them. On lap 18 Leclerc stopped waiting, went down the inside at Turn 1 and made it stick.

It mattered more than a sprint place. Leclerc had started on softs, one of only two drivers who did, and he turned that into second on a circuit where nobody overtakes.

## The tyre call

Almost the whole field started on mediums. Leclerc and [Oliver Bearman](/drivers/oliver-bearman) went for softs instead. One of them finished second. That is a small decision at a track that punishes small decisions, and Ferrari got it right on a weekend when their qualifying pace kept letting them down.

Two cars never made the grid properly. [Carlos Sainz](/drivers/carlos-sainz) and [Fernando Alonso](/drivers/fernando-alonso) both started from the pit lane after setup changes. [Sergio Pérez](/drivers/sergio-perez)'s Cadillac needed repairs in the pit lane before the formation lap had even finished, and he came home three laps down.

[Nico Hulkenberg](/drivers/nico-hulkenberg) lasted seven laps before a power unit failure ended his afternoon, which is a hard result for the Audi that had looked so composed on mediums in Friday practice. Bortoleto was investigated for an unsafe release.

## What it did to the championship

Everything and nothing. The order at the top did not change. The gaps did.

| Driver | Before the sprint | Sprint | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kimi Antonelli | 219 | +5 | **224** |
| Lewis Hamilton | 169 | +2 | **171** |
| George Russell | 160 | +8 | **168** |
| Charles Leclerc | 138 | +7 | **145** |
| Lando Norris | 128 | +6 | **134** |
| Max Verstappen | 109 | +3 | **112** |
| Oscar Piastri | 92 | +4 | **96** |

Russell took the maximum eight and is now **three points** behind [Lewis Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) in the fight for second. [Kimi Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) still leads, by 53 over Hamilton and 56 over his own team-mate, but he spent the sprint in fourth watching two cars he needs to beat score more than him.

Russell's own read afterwards was cautious rather than triumphant: "Obviously a great start to the weekend. The Ferraris look fast so it's gonna be tight this afternoon."

He was right, and not in the way he meant. Ferrari were not the problem in qualifying a few hours later. Norris was.
