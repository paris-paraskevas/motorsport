# Sixty years, and a nineteenth place: Antonelli wins at Monza for Italy

The last Italian to win a Formula 1 race at Monza was Ludovico Scarfiotti, in a Ferrari, in 1966. Nobody has managed it since, through the whole of the sport's modern history, and a fair amount of national effort has gone into trying. On Sunday a twenty-year-old from Bologna did it in a **Mercedes**, having started **nineteenth**.

[Kimi Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) won the [Italian Grand Prix](/series/f1/weekend/13) by 3.857s from his own team-mate, in the seventh victory of his season, on the one weekend of the year his team had decided in advance he would be starting near the back.

Fifteen cars finished on the lead lap. Three retired.

## Ferrari lost their home race in two laps

[Charles Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc) started third and [Lewis Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) fourth, both on softs, both aiming for the same piece of road. Hamilton went down the inside into the first chicane, Leclerc held his line, they touched, and Hamilton was in the gravel and [down to ninth](https://www.formula1.com/en/video/2026-Italian-Grand-Prix-Gasly-leads-away-as-Hamilton-drops-to-p9-on-the-race-start.1875588277934901342) before the lap was out. "Charles pushed me off," he said on the radio. No penalty followed, and the FIA published no decision document about it at all.

Then it got worse. At the end of lap two, defending fourth from [Oscar Piastri](/drivers/oscar-piastri), Leclerc got a snap of oversteer on the exit of the Parabolica and went into the outside barrier hard. He climbed out unhurt. The safety car became a red flag because the barrier needed rebuilding, and the race stopped for about half an hour.

Leclerc suggested afterwards there may have been a throttle issue, and [Ferrari](/teams/ferrari) went away to look at whether a torque spike was involved. It looked uncomfortably like his 2020 crash at the same corner.

## Hamilton wants it in writing

The fallout was the more interesting half. Hamilton said afterwards that [Ferrari have "no rules"](https://www.skysports.com/f1/news/12433/13582222/lewis-hamilton-claims-there-are-no-rules-at-ferrari-after-clash-with-team-mate-charles-leclerc-during-italian-gp) governing how their drivers race each other, that at [Mercedes](/teams/mercedes) the rules of engagement were written down, and that this needs sorting. He maintained he was ahead at the apex of Turn 2. Leclerc said the two of them had gone too far and took some of it on himself, and Hamilton declined the shared blame.

**Our view:** Hamilton is right that Ferrari should have written rules, and wrong that this incident is the argument for them. He attacked a team-mate on the opening lap at the one circuit where the car behind always has a run, and got the outcome that move usually gets. What the weekend actually exposed is that Ferrari now have two drivers who will not yield to each other and no agreed answer for what happens next. That is a problem for Madrid, not a problem for Monza.

## The nineteenth-place win

[Mercedes](/teams/mercedes) chose this. Antonelli needed a full new power unit at some point in the season, they ran the numbers at Zandvoort, and the numbers said Monza. Toto Wolff put it about as bluntly as a team principal can: the calculations picked the track, and algorithms do not take a driver's nationality into account.

What followed:

- **First start.** Nineteenth to twelfth on the opening lap, before Leclerc's crash stopped it.
- **Restart.** Twelfth on the grid, eighth by the end of the first racing lap.
- **The climb.** Past Piastri, past Hamilton, past [Max Verstappen](/drivers/max-verstappen), and then past [George Russell](/drivers/george-russell) into the first chicane on lap 18 for the lead.
- **The strategy.** A mid-race virtual safety car, and Mercedes split their cars. Antonelli took fresh mediums. Russell stayed out on used hards, which faded.
- **The scare.** Lap 49 of 53, attacking Russell at the Variante del Rettifilo, Antonelli went through the gravel and kept it.
- **The pass.** Three laps from the flag, and that one stuck.
- **The flourish.** He set the fastest lap of the race on the way to the flag, a **1:23.504 on lap 53**, the last lap of the afternoon.

Two Mercedes trading the lead of a Grand Prix for thirty laps is either a thrilling advertisement for letting drivers race or a fortnight of awkward meetings, depending on how the season finishes. At Zandvoort the same team moved Russell aside for three points. At Monza they let it run, and the one who came through the field won it.

"This is a day that I will remember forever," Antonelli said. "It is for sure the most special win of my career so far." Russell, who led much of it and lost it twice, was shorter: "It's frustrating to be so close to victory and not to achieve it but that is racing sometimes." Toto Wolff called it "a phenomenal race by the team and both drivers".

## Gasly led, and Monza took it back

[Pierre Gasly](/drivers/pierre-gasly) had the pole, the launch and the lead, and finished **seventh**. That is not a failure, it is a description of the circuit. A low-drag [Alpine](/teams/alpine) that can qualify at the front is still an Alpine on lap thirty with a Mercedes in its mirrors, and Monza gives the car behind a tow the car in front cannot do anything about.

He led the opening laps of a Grand Prix at the circuit where he won one, six years after the fact. It was a good weekend, it deserved better than seventh, and seventh was always what it was going to be.

## Behind the podium

[Lando Norris](/drivers/lando-norris) took fourth off Piastri late on once his hard tyres came in, the two of them wheel-to-wheel with Norris briefly on the grass before [McLaren](/teams/mclaren) called it off. Their gap at the flag was **0.197s** after 53 laps of Monza, and they are team-mates, which is the kind of thing that makes a pit wall reach for the radio.

[Arvid Lindblad](/drivers/arvid-lindblad) was eighth and [Franco Colapinto](/drivers/franco-colapinto) ninth. [Fernando Alonso](/drivers/fernando-alonso) retired with suspected damage and [Lance Stroll](/drivers/lance-stroll) with hydraulics, so [Aston Martin](/teams/aston-martin) scored nothing and finished neither car. Alonso had started from the pit lane, as had [Liam Lawson](/drivers/liam-lawson), both for work done on the cars under parc ferme; only twenty of the twenty-two took the start from a grid slot at all.

## One result that is not final

Yuki Tsunoda finished tenth and was summoned afterwards. On the restart he approached the grid heading for the wrong box, corrected himself, and parked correctly; the race director ordered an extra formation lap anyway because, in his own evidence, he was not comfortable starting with the car in that position. Article B5.9.4 says a driver who causes an extra formation lap must then start from the pit lane, and Tsunoda did not.

The stewards took no further action, and their reasoning turns on something small. The penalty, they wrote, "is for failing to come into the Pit Lane and start from there. The penalty is not for causing the extra formation lap." To comply, a driver would have to know he was the cause, and nobody told [Racing Bulls](/teams/racing-bulls) he was.

[Audi](/teams/audi) have lodged a **notice of intention to appeal**, which is not the same as appealing. That notice buys 96 hours to decide whether to actually file. If they file and win, an unserved stop-and-go converts to a thirty-second penalty, Tsunoda drops to fifteenth, and [Gabriel Bortoleto](/drivers/gabriel-bortoleto) inherits the final point.

We are flagging it for a reason. Three days ago the FIA's International Court of Appeal reinstated Pierre Gasly's Monaco penalties and moved five drivers in a race run in June, and our own Monaco page needed correcting to match. A classification is not final until the appeals are.

Antonelli's lead goes from 59 to **66**. He has seven of the thirteen wins this season. Russell has two, Norris two, Hamilton one, Leclerc one.

The championship is now a Mercedes internal matter with Hamilton as the outside interest, and the awkward part for [Ferrari](/teams/ferrari) is that their two drivers spent Sunday racing each other into a barrier and a gravel trap while the title went past.

## Where it leaves us

**For the books:** the first Italian winner at Monza since Ludovico Scarfiotti in 1966, a win from nineteenth on the grid, Antonelli's seventh victory of the season, Gasly's first pole and the opening laps in his hands, Leclerc into the Parabolica barrier on lap two, a red flag and a thirty-minute stoppage, a Ferrari first-lap collision the stewards let stand, and a tenth place still under appeal.

Monza spent the whole weekend being told this was Ferrari's year to have a moment at home, with the engine step and the Schumacher anniversaries and both cars in the top six of every practice session. It got an Italian win instead, in a silver car, from nineteenth on the grid, by a driver who was not supposed to be anywhere near the front. The tifosi took it. They were never going to do anything else.

Madrid next, on a circuit nobody has raced.
