# Ferrari one-two in the Monza morning, and four cars handed to somebody else

First practice at [Monza](/series/f1/weekend/13) is the least reliable hour of the weekend and the most watched one at this circuit, because for one morning a year the timing screen can be made to say the right thing. On Friday it did. [Charles Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc) put a **1:23.008** on the board, [Lewis Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) followed him 0.173s later, and [Ferrari](/teams/ferrari) headed a session at their own Grand Prix.

It meant nothing by Sunday afternoon. It was worth having anyway.

[Formula 1's own report](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/fp1-leclerc-leads-hamilton-and-russell-during-first-practice-at-the-italian-grand-prix.7DUTqVtZlb4zvqNfBsyl1t) had it the same way. [George Russell](/drivers/george-russell) was third for [Mercedes](/teams/mercedes) at +0.304 and would go on to top both of the sessions that followed, which in hindsight is the only line on this sheet that predicted anything.

## Four teams gave a car away

Look at tenth, fifteenth, seventeenth and twenty-second. Paul Aron in the [Alpine](/teams/alpine), Luke Browning in the [Williams](/teams/williams), Ayumu Iwasa in the [Red Bull](/teams/red-bull-racing) and Colton Herta in the [Cadillac](/teams/cadillac). None of them has a race seat. All four were in a car on Friday morning because the regulations say they have to be.

Every full-time driver must stand aside for a rookie in two practice sessions a season, which works out at four outings per team across the year, and Monza is a popular place to spend one. We wrote the rule up in [how a Formula 1 race weekend works](/information/formula-1/how-a-formula-1-race-weekend-works) before the weekend started, largely because it is the sort of thing that makes a Friday timing sheet unreadable if you do not know it is coming.

So [Pierre Gasly](/drivers/pierre-gasly), [Max Verstappen](/drivers/max-verstappen), [Alexander Albon](/drivers/alexander-albon) and [Sergio Perez](/drivers/sergio-perez) all watched the first hour from the garage. Gasly took pole position the next day without having turned a wheel on Friday morning, which is either a compliment to Alpine's simulator or a comment on how little this session is worth.

Herta's hour was the shortest of anyone's: **five laps** and +6.914, which is a garage problem rather than a driver one. Aron's tenth on twenty-three laps was the best of the four, and comfortably ahead of the race driver in the other Alpine on outright pace over a single lap.

## Lawson, fourth, in a car he is still learning

[Liam Lawson](/drivers/liam-lawson) was fourth. He is in the Red Bull because [Isack Hadjar](/drivers/isack-hadjar) is not, and at Zandvoort a fortnight ago he had never driven the 2026 car at all and finished the only practice session fourteenth. One weekend later he was 0.425s off the top of the sheet with the second Red Bull entry given over to Iwasa for the morning.

He started Sunday's race from the pit lane and finished fourteenth, so the Friday was the best part of his weekend. It usually is when you are the newest thing in the garage.

## What it was actually worth

Nothing, on the evidence. Ferrari's one-two became fourth and fifth in qualifying, **third and fourth on the grid**, and a retirement on the second lap of the Grand Prix. Antonelli was fifth here, seventh in qualifying, nineteenth on the grid, and won the race. Gasly did not set a lap and took pole position by the end of Saturday.

That is Monza in a sentence. The lap is so short and the tow so valuable that a Friday morning time tells you who turned up, roughly, and almost nothing else.

**For the books:** a Ferrari one-two in first practice at their home race, four rookies in four cars under the mandatory-outing rule, Colton Herta's five laps the shortest session of anybody's, and the man who would take pole the next day not turning a wheel all morning.
