# Norris steals it by a whisker, then the stewards redraw the grid: Hungary qualifying

Qualifying at the Hungaroring gave us the closest thing to a fair fight all weekend, and then handed the result to a committee. On track, [Lando Norris](/drivers/lando-norris) produced a 1:17.207 to snatch pole from [Lewis Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) by twelve thousandths of a second, his first pole of a season he is supposed to be leading. Hamilton had provisionally taken it; the reigning champion found the margin on his final run and took it back. A brilliant lap, a brilliant session, and for about an hour a genuinely mouthwatering front row.

## Then qualifying moved indoors

The stewards were busy. First, [Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) was handed a three-place grid penalty for impeding [Oscar Piastri](/drivers/oscar-piastri) at Turn 1 in Q3, the [Ferrari](/teams/ferrari) sitting on the racing line and forcing the [McLaren](/teams/mclaren) wide and off its lap. Second, championship leader [Kimi Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli), who had qualified fourth, copped his own three-place drop for failing to slow enough under the yellow flags thrown when [Max Verstappen](/drivers/max-verstappen) spun his [Red Bull](/teams/red-bull-racing) in Q3. Two separate offences, two identical sanctions, and a grid that looked nothing like the timesheet by the time the paperwork cleared.

## The grid it left behind

Norris keeps pole, untouched. The big winner is [Charles Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc), promoted onto the front row in second by Hamilton's demotion, an all-red second row becoming a McLaren-Ferrari one. [Piastri](/drivers/oscar-piastri) inherits third, [Verstappen](/drivers/max-verstappen) climbs to fourth from a genuine sixth, and Hamilton drops to fifth on a day he might have started second. [George Russell](/drivers/george-russell) lines up sixth, and Antonelli, who was fourth on merit, starts seventh, a needless self-inflicted step back for a man carrying a championship lead into the summer.

## Why any of this matters

Because it is the Hungaroring, and the Hungaroring does not do overtaking. A grid penalty here is not a minor inconvenience to be undone on Sunday; it is, more often than not, the race. Norris starts where he wants to be. Ferrari, the team everyone spent the week fancying, put a car on the front row and left another wounded in fifth. And the championship leader begins from seventh at a track where seventh tends to stay seventh. The stopwatch had its say. Then the stewards had theirs. Sunday's race, and where the [standings](/series/f1/standings) sit after it, are on the [weekend page](/series/f1/weekend/11).
