# Hamilton Leads the Ferrari one-two in a red flag ridden Hungary FP2

If first practice was a rumour, the afternoon started filling in the details. [Lewis Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) led a [Ferrari](/teams/ferrari) one-two in FP2, a tenth and a half clear of [Charles Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc), and this time the red cars were quick everywhere rather than only through the corners. The hopium has stopped being a punchline and started looking like a problem for everyone else. It was not a tidy hour: [Franco Colapinto](/drivers/franco-colapinto) put his [Alpine](/teams/alpine) in the barriers, [Lance Stroll](/drivers/lance-stroll) never made it out of the garage at all, and the championship leader spent his afternoon trying not to spin. But the top of the timesheet could not have been redder.

## Hamilton doing Hamilton things at the Hamiltonring

Hamilton has spent the best part of two decades treating the Hungaroring as a second home, and a 1:18.729 on a set of soft tyres already six laps old is the kind of lap that says he still means it. It did not come easy. Leclerc had gone quickest with a 1:18.877 inside the closing twenty minutes, only for the seven-time champion to snatch it back with the very next effort. Two Ferraris, three tenths clear of everyone else, at a circuit built to reward precisely what their car does best. For the first time this season, "Ferrari are the team to beat" is a sentence you can say without a laugh track.

## No issues for Ferrari

The FP1 sting, Leclerc coasting to a halt with a suspected gearbox, did not repeat itself. Both cars ran clean for the full hour, and the straight-line worry from the morning quietly disappeared with them: Hamilton and Leclerc went through the speed trap at 327 and 326 km/h, right in the pack rather than propping up the field the way they had before lunch. Quick through the corners was never in doubt. Quick in a straight line and reliable for a full session is how a promising Friday turns into a threat.

## Difficulty settling in for Kimi, Russell struggles to 5th

If the morning's oddity was [Kimi Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) watching a rookie warm up his car, the afternoon's was the championship leader climbing back in and going backwards. Antonelli was thirteenth, nearly two seconds off Hamilton, his session a catalogue of lock-ups, a couple of near-spins under braking and a mistake in the final sector that threw away his best lap. [Mercedes](/teams/mercedes) offered to take some front wing out to settle the rear; he was not sold. [George Russell](/drivers/george-russell) did better in fifth and still climbed out complaining of an "extremely bad" feeling from the car. The Hungaroring is doing to the silver cars exactly what everyone promised it would: removing the one thing they are best at and putting a magnifying glass over everything they are not. Forty-five points still cushion Antonelli at the top of the [standings](/series/f1/standings), and a scruffy Friday does not dent that, but it was nobody's idea of a statement.

## McLaren, as promised

McLaren mumbling through a Friday and then conjuring half a second is one of the sport's oldest routines, and right on cue, [Lando Norris](/drivers/lando-norris) hauled the [McLaren](/teams/mclaren) up to third, best of the non-Ferraris and a different postcode from the eleventh a rookie-shared FP1 had left him in. The new floor got a proper run, [Oscar Piastri](/drivers/oscar-piastri) was back in the other car in eighth, and the low-speed-corner weakness that was supposed to sink them here looked a good deal more manageable once the track warmed up. Not a Ferrari. But the closest thing to one.

## Colapinto's error

The red flag belonged to [Colapinto](/drivers/franco-colapinto). Just before half-distance the Alpine snapped away from him on the entry to the final corner and speared backwards into the barriers, wrecking the rear of the car in a stiff crosswind on a surface that had been dusty and mean all day. He climbed out unhurt; the car will need a new back end. The timing was cruel for everyone else, because the stoppage dropped right into the middle of the soft-tyre qualifying runs. Piastri and Antonelli were among those who never got a clean lap in afterwards, which is worth holding on to before anyone reads the order behind the Ferraris as gospel.

## No FP2 for Stroll, more disappointment at Aston Martin

Spare another thought for [Aston Martin](/teams/aston-martin). Their morning ended with [Stroll](/drivers/lance-stroll)'s brand-new car pitching him into a spin on a suspected rear-suspension failure. Their afternoon did not happen: the team could not repair the damage in time, so Stroll sat out FP2 completely and was classified last, having managed eleven laps of his sixteen-part gamble in the whole day. Adrian Newey, the man whose name is on the project, conceded the team were "short on spares" and that the failure was "very unexpected", which is not a sentence you want from your technical figurehead on debut day. There was a sliver of light: in [Fernando Alonso](/drivers/fernando-alonso)'s hands the new car looked a genuine step, hauling Aston back among the Cadillacs at the tail rather than stranded behind them. Progress, of a sort, measured from rock bottom and gathered on a single car.

## No Race Pace Data

The one thing FP2 could not hand over was a clean look at race pace. Colapinto's crash gutted the long-run phase, so the tyre-wear picture that usually makes a Friday worth studying is half-drawn at best. On a single fast lap the message is blunt: Ferrari, then a gap, then the rest. Over a stint, nobody can honestly say yet, and anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing.

## Where it leaves us

So Ferrari roll into Saturday as clear favourites for a pole that, at this circuit, is usually worth more than any clever strategy. [Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) has the happy memories and, today, the fastest car; [Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc) has the FP1 headline and a score to settle; between them they have turned a season of hopium into something that looks a lot like expectation. [Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) still leads by 45 and would dearly like to remember how to string a lap together before it matters. Third practice and qualifying come on Saturday, and the running order, live and in your own timezone, sits on the [weekend page](/series/f1/weekend/11). The hopium has a stopwatch behind it now. That part is new.
