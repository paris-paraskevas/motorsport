# Leclerc ends the drought as Silverstone descends into chaos — and Ferrari hit 250

Silverstone promised a Mercedes coronation and delivered a Ferrari resurrection. Charles Leclerc won a British Grand Prix that unravelled into late chaos and finished, anticlimactically, behind the Safety Car — but nothing could dull the headline. It was Leclerc's first win since the 2024 United States Grand Prix, his ninth in F1, his first at Silverstone, and, fittingly, **Ferrari's 250th victory in Formula 1** — scored 75 years after the Scuderia's very first, on this same Northamptonshire tarmac, in 1951.

## A pole that turned to heartbreak

It should have been Kimi Antonelli's day. The championship leader arrived on a high — Sprint pole had gone to Lewis Hamilton on Saturday morning, but Antonelli won the 17-lap Sprint to stretch his title lead to 43 points, then put it on pole for the Grand Prix with a 1:28.111, edging Leclerc by 0.175s with Hamilton third and a recovering George Russell fourth after an early spin at Luffield.

Then Sunday happened. Leclerc launched brilliantly, slipping ahead with Hamilton as Antonelli bogged slightly off the line. The Italian clawed his way back to second and briefly led when Leclerc pitted — but on lap 41 his car cried enough, Mercedes pointing to a left-front wheel-shield failure, compounded by a five-second track-limits penalty. From a probable win, Antonelli tumbled to 16th, out of the points, a fastest lap (1:31.777) his only souvenir. His championship lead, 43 points on Saturday night, was gone to just 25 by Sunday evening.

## Verstappen, the Safety Car, and shades of 2021

The decisive blow came on lap 48. Max Verstappen, running a strong third and eyeing the podium, spun into the gravel at Stowe — the latest chapter in a recurring Red Bull problem he later called "frustrating and painful." His beached car brought out the Safety Car, and the race never went green again. Amid confusion over lapped cars and a false promise that the Safety Car would peel in — the FIA later blamed a software error that flashed an incorrect message — the finish carried unmistakable echoes of Abu Dhabi 2021, only this time the order simply froze.

## Russell steals second at home

For George Russell it was a home race of two halves. A slow puncture had dropped him to seventh and seemingly out of the podium fight; Antonelli's failure and Verstappen's crash dragged him back into it, and by staying out while others pitted under the Safety Car he vaulted Lewis Hamilton for second. He was pointedly unimpressed with his own afternoon — but a home rostrum, and 18 points clawed back on his runaway teammate, will have softened it.

Hamilton completed the podium the hard way: a five-second penalty for a false start, then a post-race summons for a yellow-flag infringement that earned only a reprimand. Third on home soil, in red, he took it. Lando Norris salvaged McLaren's difficult weekend in fourth, while teammate Oscar Piastri never recovered from lap-one front-wing damage and trailed home 11th, out of the points.

## The rest of the day

Behind the leaders, Isack Hadjar led Red Bull's charge to fifth, and Racing Bulls banked a double score through Liam Lawson and Arvid Lindblad in sixth and seventh — enough to close to within a single point of Alpine, whose Franco Colapinto and Pierre Gasly took ninth and tenth around Gabriel Bortoleto's Audi in eighth. Carlos Sainz's promising run ended pointless in 12th after a Safety Car breach cost him a lap. Verstappen, Alex Albon and Nico Hülkenberg were the retirements.

**Top ten:** 1. Leclerc (Ferrari), 2. Russell (Mercedes), 3. Hamilton (Ferrari), 4. Norris (McLaren), 5. Hadjar (Red Bull), 6. Lawson (Racing Bulls), 7. Lindblad (Racing Bulls), 8. Bortoleto (Audi), 9. Colapinto (Alpine), 10. Gasly (Alpine).

## Where it leaves us

Antonelli still leads the championship, but a 43-point cushion is now 25, and the pressure — from his own teammate — is suddenly real. Ferrari, meanwhile, leave Silverstone with a landmark win and a driver reborn: "It feels incredible," said Leclerc. "To win after the last few weekends that have been particularly difficult…" The sentence trailed off, but the message didn't.

The season takes a weekend off and returns at the **Belgian Grand Prix on 17–19 July** — Spa, where chaos needs no invitation.
