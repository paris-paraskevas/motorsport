# Nine years for six hundredths: Gasly puts an Alpine on pole at Monza

[Pierre Gasly](/drivers/pierre-gasly) had been to Monza before and left with something. In 2020 he won here, from tenth, in a Toro Rosso, in the strangest race of that season. On Saturday he came back and took the first pole position of his career at the same circuit, and did it with a lap nobody in the paddock had any reason to expect.

**1:21.786.** Six hundredths of a second clear of a Mercedes that had led both of the sessions that mattered. He had been fourteenth on Friday afternoon and ninth that morning.

"It's been nine years trying to chase and get the opportunity to take pole position," he [told Formula 1 afterwards](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/gasly-charges-to-sensational-maiden-f1-pole-at-monza-over-russell-and-piastri.4CKkkbvgmqL04ijMNBfuXF). He was third after the opening Q3 runs and produced the lap on the final one.

## The man who stopped trying, and started nineteenth

Look at Q2 rather than Q3. [Kimi Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) went **1:21.882** in the middle segment, quicker than anybody else managed in Q2 and quicker than eight of the ten Q3 laps that followed. Then in Q3 he went **1:22.093**, two tenths slower than his own Q2 time, qualified seventh, and started the race **nineteenth**.

A driver does not go slower in the segment that decides the grid by accident. He already knew where he was starting.

[Mercedes](/teams/mercedes) had decided at Zandvoort to take a full new power unit for him here, which carries a grid penalty, and to do it at the race he would most want to start from the front. Toto Wolff had explained the arithmetic without dressing it up: the calculations said Monza was the best track to spend it, and algorithms do not take a driver's nationality into account.

Antonelli's own read was that starting at the back "in some ways helps to release pressure", though he would very much rather have been fighting for pole. He thought a top five was realistic.

## What the grid actually looked like

Qualifying and the grid are two different documents, and at Monza they disagreed badly. The FIA's [final starting grid](/series/f1/weekend/13/qualifying) carried five penalties:

| Car | Penalty | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Antonelli | **30 places** | additional power unit elements |
| Albon | 20 places | additional power unit elements |
| Lawson | 35 places, then **pit lane** | additional power unit elements, then car modified under parc ferme |
| Alonso | **pit lane** | car modified under parc ferme, additional power unit elements |
| Piastri | 3 places | impeding |

Which turned the order into this:

The front row stood, the next four shuffled, and only twenty cars lined up on the grid at all because two of them started from the pit lane.

## Piastri lost three places for getting in Lawson's way

The one penalty here that was not about engines is worth reading, because the stewards did something slightly unusual with it. [Oscar Piastri](/drivers/oscar-piastri) impeded [Liam Lawson](/drivers/liam-lawson) at Turn 2 during qualifying, a breach of Article B4.1.1, and took the standard three-place drop.

What makes it interesting is the reasoning. On leaving the pits Piastri was told over the radio to watch for Verstappen and Lawson as he joined, but not that they were on push laps. He worked out Verstappen was coming, slowed, kept right and let him through, and could do nothing about Lawson behind. The stewards accepted that he "had, at this stage, no other options available" and that the impeding was inadvertent, and gave him the three places anyway, because the penalty for impeding is a standard one and intention does not enter into it.

So the driver who qualified third started sixth for a mistake the stewards agreed he could not avoid. That is the rule working exactly as written, and it is still hard to look at.

## Ferrari, fourth and fifth, at home

[Charles Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc) and [Lewis Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) were separated by **0.007s** and lined up third and fourth once the penalties shook out. For a team that had been in the top six of all three practice sessions with a new engine step, second row at their own Grand Prix was a reasonable return and an underwhelming one at the same time.

Hamilton has more pole positions at Monza than anyone. He did not add to them, and neither Ferrari saw the end of the race in the position it started.

## Our view

Gasly's lap is the best single piece of driving of the weekend and it will be remembered for about a fortnight, because of what happened on Sunday. That is the shape of a Monza pole: it is worth an enormous amount on Saturday evening and rather less at four o'clock the following afternoon, at a circuit where the slipstream gives the car behind a weapon the car in front cannot answer.

He led the opening laps. He finished seventh.

**For the books:** Gasly's first pole in nine years of trying and at the circuit of his first win, both Alpines in the top eight, ten cars inside half a second in Q3, Leclerc and Hamilton split by seven thousandths, and the fastest Q2 lap of anybody belonging to a driver who started nineteenth.
