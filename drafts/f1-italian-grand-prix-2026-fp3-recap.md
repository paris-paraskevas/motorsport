# Russell makes it two from two, and the top eight are covered by half a second

Saturday morning at Monza is the last hour before it counts. Parc fermé closes at the end of it, the fuel loads come down, and the timing screen finally starts telling something close to the truth. What it said was that [Mercedes](/teams/mercedes) were the quickest thing at the circuit and that seven other cars were near enough to make qualifying a lottery.

Both halves of that turned out to be true. Neither produced the result anyone expected.

**0.554s** covering the top eight, and five different teams inside it. [Russell](/drivers/george-russell) set his time on **thirteen laps**, fewer than anybody else in the top twelve, which is the sort of detail that reads as confidence.

## Two drivers who already knew where they were starting

[Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) was fourth and [Alexander Albon](/drivers/alexander-albon) eighteenth, and both of them were going to line up at the back on Sunday for taking new engines. Albon lost part of the session to a steering wheel problem that had the [Williams](/teams/williams) wheeled back into the garage, then spent the end of it towing [Carlos Sainz](/drivers/carlos-sainz) down the straight, and [both were investigated afterwards](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/fp3-russell-goes-fastest-from-hamilton-and-verstappen-in-final-italy-practice.6i9o4gazCqUo3UtLVRgoe8) over a yellow flag.

There was needle elsewhere too. [Verstappen](/drivers/max-verstappen) complained about a Ferrari being permanently in his way, Russell was looked at for impeding [Piastri](/drivers/oscar-piastri) and cleared, and Hamilton and Verstappen went side by side down the main straight and arrived at Turn 2 in a mood. On a lap this short with twenty-two cars looking for a tow, Saturday morning at Monza is mostly a traffic-management exercise.

## Gasly, ninth, and about to embarrass everybody

Ninth in final practice. Fourteenth on Friday afternoon. Then a **1:21.786** in Q3 and the first pole position of his career.

There is no reading of this session that saw it coming, and it is worth being honest about that rather than reverse-engineering a signal. What [Alpine](/teams/alpine) had was a car set up for one thing, at the one circuit where that thing is the whole lap, and a driver who put it together when the fuel came out. Practice does not reward that. Qualifying does.

## Ferrari's last good session

[Lewis Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) second and [Charles Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc) sixth meant [Ferrari](/teams/ferrari) had been in the top six of all three practice sessions at their home Grand Prix. Hamilton has won at this circuit five times, a record he shares with Michael Schumacher, and this was the weekend Ferrari marked thirty years since Schumacher joined them and twenty since his last Monza win for them.

They qualified fourth and fifth. Leclerc was in the barriers at the Parabolica on lap two and Hamilton finished sixth. The tifosi got Saturday morning and not much else.

## What it actually set up

Three sessions, two different leaders, and a Q3 order that matched none of them. [Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) was fourth here and set the fastest lap of anybody in Q2, and started the race nineteenth because of a power unit penalty his team took deliberately at his home race. [Verstappen](/drivers/max-verstappen) was third here and third on Sunday, the only driver all weekend whose Saturday morning form survived contact with the result.

If you want the mechanics of why a Saturday morning at Monza predicts so little, the format itself is laid out in [how a Formula 1 race weekend works](/information/formula-1/how-a-formula-1-race-weekend-works).

**For the books:** Russell's second session win of the weekend on thirteen laps, the top eight inside 0.554s across five teams, Ferrari in the top six of every practice session at their home race, and the eventual polesitter ninth.
