# Mercedes turn up in the afternoon, and the man who takes pole is fourteenth

The tow makes Monza lie. Get a run in behind somebody on a quiet lap and you find three tenths that are not really yours; take your lap in clean air and you look slower than you are. Second practice on Friday afternoon is where that distortion is at its worst, because everybody is chasing a low-fuel number at once.

With that health warning attached: [George Russell](/drivers/george-russell) went quickest, and by Saturday lunchtime he had done it again.

Then Colapinto eleventh, Lawson twelfth, Hulkenberg thirteenth, **Gasly fourteenth**, Bortoleto fifteenth, Albon sixteenth, Sainz seventeenth, Ocon eighteenth, Alonso nineteenth, Perez twentieth, Bottas twenty-first and Stroll twenty-second.

The whole field inside **2.694s**, and the top six inside half a second. Monza compresses everything, which is why the circuit keeps producing qualifying sessions decided by hundredths and races decided by something else entirely.

All twenty-two regular drivers were back in the cars, [Formula 1 noted](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/fp2-russell-beats-leclerc-and-antonelli-to-the-fastest-time-in-free-practice-2-ahead-of-italian-grand-prix.4mVLF757oe0NuWE9Caw1wO), after four of them sat out the morning for rookies. [Russell](/drivers/george-russell) aborted his first run on softs to come in for a front wing adjustment and the lap he set afterwards was never beaten. [Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc) spun at the second chicane inside the last ten minutes and rejoined without damage. [Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) spent his afternoon complaining about understeer and was still third.

## The three names worth marking

[Pierre Gasly](/drivers/pierre-gasly) was **fourteenth**, 1.214s off Russell, in an [Alpine](/teams/alpine) that had shown nothing all day. Twenty-four hours later he was on pole position at this circuit by six hundredths of a second. Nothing in this session forecast that, and anybody claiming otherwise is reading backwards.

[Max Verstappen](/drivers/max-verstappen) was ninth, which for [Red Bull](/teams/red-bull-racing) at a low-drag circuit is worse than it sounds. He finished the race third.

[Kimi Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) was third, 0.141s off his own team-mate, and had the second-largest lap count in the session at thirty-two. That mattered more than the time. He already knew he was taking a full new power unit and starting near the back on Sunday, so Friday afternoon was not about a single lap for him. It was about learning what the car would do in traffic on a circuit where he would be spending the whole afternoon in it.

## Ferrari were still there

[Charles Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc) second and [Lewis Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) fifth, so [Ferrari](/teams/ferrari) had both cars in the top five for the second session running. That is a genuinely good Friday by the standards of their season, and it came with an engine step brought specifically for this weekend under the in-season upgrade allowance the 2026 rules provide.

They qualified fourth and fifth. Leclerc did not complete the second lap of the race.

## The bit that never shows up on the sheet

Ten teams declared **26 new parts** to the FIA for this weekend, and eleven of those, from six teams, were filed under drag range or drag reduction. Friday afternoon is when you find out whether taking the wings off actually worked, and the answer at Monza is always the same: the low-drag cars look quick in a straight line and vague everywhere else, and the lap time lands wherever the compromise lands.

You can read the full declaration on the [technical file for the weekend](/series/f1/weekend/13). It is the most honest document of any Grand Prix, because teams have to say what they changed and why.

**For the books:** Russell's first of two, the field covered by 2.694s, Gasly fourteenth on the Friday before his maiden pole, Verstappen ninth, and both Ferraris in the top five for the second session in a row.
