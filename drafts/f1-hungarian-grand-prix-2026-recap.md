# The sandbag paid off: Norris sweeps Hungary as McLaren remember the plot

Two Fridays ago we said it out loud: [McLaren](/teams/mclaren) mumbling their way through a Friday and then producing half a second on Saturday is one of the sport's oldest routines. Turns out they had the whole weekend in mind. [Lando Norris](/drivers/lando-norris) sat eleventh in first practice, said nothing, then went out and took FP3, [pole](/series/f1/weekend/11) and the win. His first victory of a season he is supposed to be defending, delivered at the one track everyone had handed to somebody else.

## He had to be given it, mind

Let us not pretend it was clean. Norris started on pole and lost the lead before Turn 2, [Oscar Piastri](/drivers/oscar-piastri) diving down the inside on lap one and refusing to give it back. For half the race the reigning champion sat in his own team-mate's mirrors while McLaren, to their credit or their cowardice depending on your seat, declined to intervene. Then Hungary did what Hungary does to a leader who cannot quite escape: Piastri caught traffic, tangled with [Carlos Sainz](/drivers/carlos-sainz)'s [Williams](/teams/williams) in the first sector, and lost exactly the sliver of time Norris needed. Lap 40, an undercut, and the lead changed hands without a wheel banged.

Piastri's afternoon then unravelled entirely, his McLaren stopping at Turn 3 with a gearbox gone and bringing out the virtual safety car. Norris pitted under it, kept the lead he had inherited, and cleared off. He crossed the line 15.080 seconds clear, having been told only to keep off the kerbs and mind the white lines on the way. A first win of the year that arrived by pace, patience and one enormous slice of team-mate misfortune.

## Best of the rest, and it was the Dutchman

Behind the runaway McLaren, the podium belonged to the two men nobody expected to be up there. [Max Verstappen](/drivers/max-verstappen) hauled a car he keeps describing as a handful into second, 15 seconds back but comfortably the best of the mortals, his third rostrum in four races and, by his own admission, a genuine surprise. [Kimi Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) took the last step, which is not a bad Sunday for a championship leader who spent Friday watching a rookie warm his seat and then locked up his way through second practice. The kid is having a season.

## Ferrari's hopium, finally spent

All week the story was that the Hungaroring would make a Ferrari look reborn. For a Saturday it nearly did: [Lewis Hamilton](/drivers/lewis-hamilton) put the Scuderia within twelve thousandths of pole and led an all-red second row alongside [Charles Leclerc](/drivers/charles-leclerc). Then Sunday arrived and did the accounting. Leclerc came home fourth, Hamilton fifth after a needless five-second penalty for speeding in the pit lane in the closing laps, a self-inflicted demotion on a day that was already slipping. The corner-carving car that was meant to win here finished 24 seconds behind a McLaren that could barely see the road on Friday. If Ferrari cannot win the Hungaroring, the promising ones are running out.

## Small mercies and quiet wins

[George Russell](/drivers/george-russell) triggered his own anti-stall off the line, tumbled backwards and spent the afternoon recovering to seventh, which is roughly where the day started going wrong for [Mercedes](/teams/mercedes)' number-two seat. [Isack Hadjar](/drivers/isack-hadjar) quietly banked sixth, the French prodigy doing French-prodigy things again. And spare a cheer for [Nico Hülkenberg](/drivers/nico-hulkenberg), ninth and onto the board for the first time all year, [Audi](/teams/audi)'s veteran finally getting something for a season of trying. [Arvid Lindblad](/drivers/arvid-lindblad) rounded out the points in tenth. Sainz collected a five-second penalty for the Piastri contact, [Oliver Bearman](/drivers/oliver-bearman) the same for ignoring blue flags, and [Sergio Pérez](/drivers/sergio-perez) and [Valtteri Bottas](/drivers/valtteri-bottas) parked two more [Cadillac](/teams/cadillac)s before the flag.

## Where it leaves us

**Top ten:** 1. Norris (McLaren), 2. Verstappen (Red Bull), 3. Antonelli (Mercedes), 4. Leclerc (Ferrari), 5. Hamilton (Ferrari), 6. Hadjar (Red Bull), 7. Russell (Mercedes), 8. [Liam Lawson](/drivers/liam-lawson) (Racing Bulls), 9. Hülkenberg (Audi), 10. Lindblad (Racing Bulls). ([Full classification](/series/f1/results).)

[Antonelli](/drivers/kimi-antonelli) keeps the [championship lead](/series/f1/standings) into the summer break, which is exactly the comfortable cushion he wanted to sit on for three weeks, even if third place is not the statement second practice threatened to deny him. Norris leaves Hungary with the win he needed and the reminder that when this McLaren works, it is still the fastest thing out there. Then the grid disappears until Zandvoort on 23 August. The sandbag paid off. The question for everyone chasing is whether it was the track, the tyres or the truth.
