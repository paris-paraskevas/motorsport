We said it, and it happened. Max Verstappen won the Singapore Grand Prix at the tenth attempt, and he did it despite radio problems that left him short of help from the pit wall. Before the weekend he called this place "unfinished business". After second places here in 2018, 2024 and 2025, it is finished: the Sprint on Saturday, pole, and now the Grand Prix, Red Bull's first win at Marina Bay since Sergio Pérez in 2022. Sochi is now the only circuit he has raced at more than twice without winning.

The start we worried about

We warned you about his starts, and for a moment it looked as if we were right. Charles Leclerc got the better getaway from second, but Verstappen held him off into Turn 1 by the smallest of margins, and the top six stayed as they started. Leclerc stayed within touching distance until a virtual safety car for Gabriel Bortoleto's stricken Audi, then began losing half a second a lap. By lap 18 he was four seconds down.

Verstappen stopped on lap 32 and rejoined in the lead. Leclerc switched back to softs under a second virtual safety car, for Lance Stroll's Aston Martin, and cut the gap to 8.3 seconds, but he never got closer. Verstappen won by 7.3 seconds, making it 12 wins from pole in 17 Singapore Grands Prix.

Hamilton's very long stint

Lewis Hamilton took the road less travelled. He started on mediums while those around him were on softs, was the first to pit on lap 18, and then stayed out on used softs under the second virtual safety car while Leclerc and Antonelli stopped. That left him on a 44-lap stint, and it caught up with him. Leclerc, ten seconds behind after his second stop, caught and passed him in five laps, and in the closing stages Hamilton slid all the way to sixth, behind both McLarens.

Mercedes: a podium, a leak and no brakes

Kimi Antonelli made third place the hard way. On lap 32 he told Mercedes, "I feel something leaking between my feet," and later, "I just went straight! The pedal was so long!" He also spent the evening flirting with a five-second penalty for track limits. He still passed Hamilton and Lando Norris late on, and third place stretches his lead over George Russell to 102 points, enough to give him a first chance to clinch the title in Austin.

Russell did exactly what we hoped he could. From 19th on the grid he fought his way to ninth, and Isack Hadjar, who started from the pit lane, joined him in the points in tenth.

The rest of the field

Oliver Bearman was the entertainment of the early laps, though he finished 12th. Pierre Gasly fell out of the points on lap 59 and finished 11th, while Liam Lawson took seventh for Racing Bulls and Nico Hülkenberg eighth for Audi. Oscar Piastri recovered from a slow puncture to finish fifth, one place behind Norris. Stroll, Franco Colapinto and Bortoleto did not finish.

What it means

Two Grands Prix in a row for Verstappen, Sepang and now Singapore, plus a Sprint, and a car that suddenly looks at home everywhere. He is too far back to matter in the title fight, but nobody will want him in their mirrors in Austin on 25 October. Antonelli arrives there knowing a strong weekend could end it; Russell knows he needs something special.
