The Formula 1 calendar turns over more than it used to. Twenty-four slots are the practical ceiling, demand to host a race is the highest it has ever been, and the result is that a venue now leaves for every one that arrives. Here is what is actually confirmed, and by whom.

## Leaving: Zandvoort, after 2026

The **Dutch Grand Prix at Circuit Zandvoort** is the clearest departure. Formula 1 and the promoter announced a **one-year extension covering 2026 and nothing beyond it**, and F1's own announcement is explicit that the decision not to continue came from the promoter: a privately owned business weighing the cost of hosting against everything else it carries. F1 says alternating years and other formats were discussed before the decision was respected.

So the 2026 race, **21-23 August**, is the last. Two details make the send-off unusual: it is the **first sprint weekend Zandvoort has ever staged**, and it is Max Verstappen's final home race, on a calendar he has won three times since the event returned in 2021 after a 36-year absence.

## Already gone: Imola

**Imola** does not appear on the 2026 calendar at all. The Emilia-Romagna Grand Prix made way for Madrid, so Italy is down to a single round at Monza. Imola's name keeps surfacing in contingency talk, which is a measure of how useful a European circuit on standby has become, not of a signed return.

## Arriving in 2026: Madrid

**Madrid** is the newcomer, round 14 on 11-13 September, on a circuit built around the IFEMA exhibition centre. It also takes the **Spanish Grand Prix** name, which is why Barcelona appears on the 2026 calendar as the Barcelona-Catalunya Grand Prix rather than as Spain's race.

## Returning in 2027: Portimão and Istanbul Park

Two circuits come back, and both are officially confirmed rather than rumoured.

- **Autódromo Internacional do Algarve (Portimão)** returns for **2027 and 2028**, under an agreement with the Portuguese government, Turismo de Portugal and the promoter Parkalgar. It last held races in 2020 and 2021, both won by Lewis Hamilton, and it takes the calendar slot Zandvoort leaves behind.
- **Istanbul Park** returns **from 2027 on a five-year agreement running to 2031**, announced in April 2026 with Türkiye's Ministry of Youth and Sports. Turkey has held nine Grands Prix; the most recent, in 2021, went to Valtteri Bottas, and the 2020 race is where Hamilton sealed his seventh title.

## Alternating rather than leaving: Spa and Barcelona

Not every gap is a goodbye. **Spa-Francorchamps and Barcelona now share a slot**, which is a newer answer to the twenty-four-race ceiling than dropping a venue outright.

| Season | Spa-Francorchamps | Barcelona-Catalunya |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 | ✓ | ✓ |
| 2027 | ✓ | – |
| 2028 | – | ✓ |
| 2029 | ✓ | – |
| 2030 | – | ✓ |
| 2031 | ✓ | – |
| 2032 | – | ✓ |

Spa's extension covers four of six years from 2026; Barcelona's runs on the even years to 2032. A circuit missing from one year's calendar is therefore not necessarily on its way out.

## The 2026 exception: a Grand Prix that moved country

One change this season belongs in a category of its own. The **Saudi Arabian Grand Prix was cancelled** and the **Bahrain Grand Prix was cancelled at Sakhir**, both following the outbreak of conflict in the region. Bahrain's race was then reinstated somewhere else entirely: it runs at the **Sepang International Circuit in Malaysia on 2-4 October**, still under the Bahrain Grand Prix name, with Bahrain keeping the commercial rights to it.

That is the first Formula 1 race at Sepang since 2017, and it is the reason the 2026 season has 23 rounds rather than 22.

## What has not been announced

The **full 2027 calendar has not been published**. Portimão and Istanbul Park are confirmed as venues and Spa is confirmed for that year, but round numbers and dates are not set, and any calendar order circulating before F1 publishes it is a draft rather than a schedule. Paddock's own [F1 calendar](/calendar?s=f1) carries dates only once they are official.
