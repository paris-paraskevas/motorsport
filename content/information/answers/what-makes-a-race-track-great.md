Ask drivers and fans and the same qualities come up again and again. A great race track tends to combine:

- **A real challenge** — fast, committed corners that punish mistakes and reward bravery. Legendary sequences like Spa's Eau Rouge–Raidillon or Suzuka's Esses separate the best from the rest.
- **Elevation and flow** — gradient changes and a rhythm that links corners together make a lap satisfying to drive and to watch.
- **Overtaking opportunities** — heavy braking zones after long straights that let a following car attack, so racing is decided on track rather than only in the pits.
- **Character and history** — a signature identity and decades of memorable moments give a circuit weight; Monaco, Monza and the Nürburgring Nordschleife all trade on heritage.
- **A margin for error that still bites** — modern safety demands run-off, but the best circuits keep a sense of consequence.

Balancing all of this against safety is the art of circuit design. Explore the venues themselves in our [tracks directory](/information/tracks).
