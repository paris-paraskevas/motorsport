**The 24 Hours of Le Mans runs three classes of car on the same track at the same time:** the top **Hypercar** prototypes fighting for the overall win, the **LMP2** prototypes, and the production-based **LMGT3** grand-touring cars. Each class has its own winner, while the fastest Hypercar takes the outright victory. Sharing one circuit between very different cars, so that quicker prototypes are always working through slower GT traffic, is the defining feature of endurance racing.

## The classes at Le Mans today

Modern Le Mans, and the wider [FIA World Endurance Championship](/series/wec) it belongs to, is built around three categories:

- **Hypercar** is the premier class and the only one racing for the overall win.
- **LMP2** is a spec prototype class, quick but a clear step below Hypercar.
- **LMGT3** is the production-based GT class, introduced in 2024.

The full WEC season now runs just two of these, Hypercar and LMGT3. LMP2 returns as a third class for the 24 Hours of Le Mans itself, which is one reason the Le Mans grid is larger than a normal race weekend. The 2026 race, for example, lined up 62 cars: 18 Hypercars, 19 LMP2s and 25 LMGT3s.

## Hypercar: the class fighting for the overall win

Hypercar is the top category, introduced in 2021 to replace the old LMP1 class. These are purpose-built hybrid prototypes, and whichever one covers the most distance in 24 hours wins the race outright.

What makes Hypercar unusual is that it is made up of two different rulebooks that race together as one class: **LMH** (Le Mans Hypercar) and **LMDh** (Le Mans Daytona h). LMH cars are engineered almost entirely in-house by the manufacturer, while LMDh cars are built on one of four approved spec chassis. A Balance of Performance system adjusts the two so either can win. There is a full breakdown in [what are LMH and LMDh](/information/endurance/what-are-lmh-and-lmdh).

Hypercar entries are run by manufacturer teams with all-professional driver line-ups. The 2026 field featured brands such as Ferrari, Toyota, Cadillac, BMW, Peugeot, Alpine and Aston Martin, with Genesis joining as a new entrant. The exact roster changes from season to season as manufacturers come and go.

## LMP2: the spec prototype class

LMP2 (Le Mans Prototype 2) sits below Hypercar. It is a single specification for everyone: a closed-cockpit prototype built only for racing, using a common Oreca 07 chassis and a Gibson V8 engine. Because the hardware is fixed, LMP2 rewards team execution and driver skill rather than manufacturer budgets, which makes it popular with independent and customer teams.

LMP2 line-ups must include at least one Silver or Bronze rated driver, so the class mixes professionals with talented amateurs and young drivers on the way up. Cars fielding a Bronze driver form their own **LMP2 Pro/Am** ranking. For how these categories work, see [what GT driver ratings mean](/information/endurance/what-do-gt-driver-ratings-mean).

Since 2024, LMP2 is no longer part of the full WEC championship. It now appears once a year, at the 24 Hours of Le Mans, as a dedicated class for that race.

## LMGT3: the production-based GT class

LMGT3 is the GT class, and the closest thing on the grid to cars you can recognise from the road. It is based on the global **FIA GT3** platform, and it replaced the previous GTE (GTE-Am) technical rules in 2024. To be eligible, a manufacturer has to build more than 2,500 road-going cars a year, which is why the class draws so many familiar brands.

The 2026 Le Mans field included LMGT3 machinery from Ferrari, Porsche, Aston Martin, BMW, Corvette, Ford, Lexus, McLaren and Mercedes-AMG, making it the largest class in the race. The cars carry Le Mans-specific additions such as luminescent number panels and leader lights that show each car's position within its class.

LMGT3 is a customer, pro-am category. Each line-up pairs a paying amateur (a Bronze or Silver rated driver) with professionals, so a gentleman driver can share a car with a factory ace. That again comes down to the [driver rating system](/information/endurance/what-do-gt-driver-ratings-mean).

## How several classes share one track

All three classes run at once, for the full 24 hours, on the same Circuit de la Sarthe of roughly 13.6 km. A Hypercar is many seconds a lap faster than an LMGT3, so the leaders lap the slower cars many times over a day and a night. Managing that traffic, picking the right moment to pass a slower car without losing time or causing a crash, is one of the central skills of endurance racing, and it runs both ways: GT drivers must stay predictable and yield cleanly to faster cars. The leader lights on the bodywork help drivers and fans see who leads each class at a glance.

## One race, several winners

Because the classes are scored separately, Le Mans produces several winners from a single race. Each class has its own podium, and the crew that leads its category to the finish wins that class. On top of that, the fastest car overall, always a Hypercar, is the outright winner of the 24 Hours of Le Mans.

Across the wider [World Endurance Championship](/series/wec) season the same split applies: the Hypercar contest carries an FIA World Championship for its drivers and manufacturers, while LMGT3 races for an FIA Endurance Trophy.

## The classes at Le Mans in short

- **Hypercar:** top prototypes (LMH and LMDh together), professional line-ups, racing for the overall win.
- **LMP2:** one spec prototype for all, pro-am line-ups, a Le Mans-only class since 2024.
- **LMGT3:** production-based GT3 cars, customer pro-am teams, replaced GTE in 2024.
- **The idea:** several classes, one track, each with its own winner, and the fastest Hypercar takes the outright victory.
