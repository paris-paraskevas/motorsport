**DRS stands for Drag Reduction System.** It was Formula 1's main overtaking aid from **2011 to 2025**: a driver-operated flap in the rear wing that opened on the straights to cut aerodynamic drag, letting a chasing car reach a higher top speed and pass the car ahead. F1 **retired DRS at the end of 2025**; for the current rules see [what replaced DRS in 2026](/information/formula-1/what-replaced-drs-manual-override-mode).

## How DRS worked

When a driver pressed the DRS button, a flap in the rear wing lifted open. That reduced the wing's downforce and, with it, its drag, so the car accelerated harder and reached a higher top speed down the straight. The effect was worth several km/h and typically a few tenths of a second, usually enough to draw alongside the car in front by the braking zone. The flap closed automatically the instant the driver touched the brakes, restoring full downforce for the corner.

## DRS zones and the detection point

DRS could only be opened inside a designated **DRS zone**, a section of straight marked on each circuit. Just before each zone sat a **detection point**, a line where the system measured the gap between cars. Most tracks had one to three DRS zones; a long circuit like Baku or Melbourne used several.

## The one-second rule

DRS was not free to use at will. A driver could only activate it in a zone if they had been recorded **within one second** of the car ahead at that zone's detection point. This is why DRS helped attacking, not defending: it was handed to the chasing car to offset the "dirty air" that makes following closely so hard in modern F1.

## When DRS could not be used

- **Not in the first laps.** DRS was disabled for the opening two racing laps of a Grand Prix (and after safety-car restarts) to keep the early scramble fair.
- **Not in the wet or when unsafe.** Race control switched DRS off in wet or low-grip conditions, since the sudden loss of rear downforce could pitch a car into a spin.
- **Not through corners.** It closed the moment the driver braked, so it only ever helped on straights.

## Why F1 introduced DRS

DRS arrived in **2011** as a response to a familiar problem: F1 cars generate so much turbulent "dirty air" that a following car loses downforce and struggles to stay close enough to attempt a pass. DRS was a deliberate, if artificial, lever to make overtaking more likely and races less processional. Fans debated it for years, some seeing it as a fair equaliser and others as a gimmick that produced "easy" passes.

## Why F1 replaced DRS for 2026

For 2026 the rules changed the cars fundamentally, and DRS was dropped. Its drag-cutting job passed to **active aerodynamics** (movable front and rear wings with a low-drag "Straight Mode"), and the overtaking help became **Overtake**, an electrical boost handed to a car running within a second of the one ahead. Together they replace what DRS used to do. There is a full breakdown in [what replaced DRS in 2026](/information/formula-1/what-replaced-drs-manual-override-mode) and [what's new in F1 for 2026](/information/formula-1/whats-new-in-f1-2026).

## DRS in short

- **What:** an adjustable rear-wing flap that cut drag on the straights.
- **When:** 2011 to 2025 (retired for 2026).
- **Where:** only inside marked DRS zones, and only within one second of the car ahead.
- **Now:** replaced by active aero plus the electric Overtake boost.
