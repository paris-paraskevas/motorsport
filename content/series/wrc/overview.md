---
title: WRC — overview
---

The FIA World Rally Championship sends four-wheel-drive **Rally1** cars — turbocharged, around 380bhp — flat out against the clock on closed public roads. Toyota, Hyundai and M-Sport Ford run the factory teams. Unlike circuit racing, crews start one at a time over timed **special stages**, and the surface changes completely from round to round: gravel, tarmac, snow and ice.

## Rally shape

Each rally runs across three or four days and fifteen-plus timed stages — up to around 350km of competitive running — with the rest of the route driven on ordinary roads in between. The final stage of each rally is the **Power Stage**, run live, with bonus points for the five fastest crews.

## Points

The overall rally classification pays the top ten **25-17-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1**. On top of that, the five fastest over **Super Sunday**'s stages score **5-4-3-2-1**, and the **Power Stage** pays another **5-4-3-2-1** — so a flawless weekend is worth 35.

## What to watch for in 2026

Fourteen rounds span four continents, from **Rallye Monte-Carlo** in January to a **Saudi Arabia** finale in November — roughly nine gravel events, four on tarmac, and Sweden's snow-and-ice round. 2026 is the **last year for the current Rally1 cars**: a cheaper, Rally2-based rules reset lands in 2027, so teams get a little extra development freedom for this final season.
