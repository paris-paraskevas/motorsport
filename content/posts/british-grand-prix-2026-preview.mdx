Round 9 takes Formula 1 to Silverstone for a **sprint weekend**, with lights out at **15:00 BST on Sunday 5 July**. Five British drivers, a brand-new breed of car, and a championship nobody predicted — here's the lay of the land.

## The title picture

Kimi Antonelli didn't just adapt to F1's 2026 rules — he ran away with them. The Mercedes driver leads on **171 points**, 40 clear of team-mate George Russell and 46 up on Lewis Hamilton, and Mercedes have won **seven of eight races**, leading Ferrari by 98 in the constructors'.

But the gap is shrinking. Antonelli's five-win streak (China, Japan, Miami, Canada, Monaco) ended with a DNF in Spain, and a scrappy Austria left him third — where Russell pounced to end his own winless run and arrive at his home race with the momentum.

The strangest line on the sheet: reigning world champion **Lando Norris sits fifth**. McLaren, dominant on the way to his 2025 crown, have slipped to third — behind only Mercedes and Ferrari — in the first year of the new rules. Norris is also the defending British GP winner, so a repeat would be some upset.

## A very British Silverstone

Five home drivers line up: Russell (P2), Hamilton (P3), Norris (P5), Oliver Bearman (Haas) and rookie Arvid Lindblad (Racing Bulls) — the most British drivers on the grid in over 20 years. Russell is many pundits' pick after Austria. Hamilton — a record **nine-time** Silverstone winner — chases a 10th, with Ferrari bringing a revised active rear wing (the "Macarena") and a low-drag diffuser to a circuit that will test their power-unit deficit.

## No DRS, new questions

2026 scrapped DRS. In its place: **active aero** (the wings flip between "Corner" and "Straight" mode) plus an **"Overtake" mode** that hands a battery boost to any driver within a second of the car ahead. Silverstone's flat-out corners barely let the cars recharge — Max Verstappen said the energy rules make the place feel "like a different track." His Red Bull took second in Austria, its best result of 2026; whether that pace holds at high-speed Silverstone is one of the weekend's big unknowns. (Driver-market chatter around Verstappen also rumbles on — treat it as paddock rumour for now.)

## The format & conditions

It's Silverstone's first sprint since 2021 — just one practice session before it counts:

- **Fri 3 Jul** — FP1 12:30 · Sprint Qualifying 16:30
- **Sat 4 Jul** — Sprint 12:00 · Qualifying 16:00
- **Sun 5 Jul** — Grand Prix 15:00 (52 laps)

(All times BST.) Unusually for Silverstone, it should stay warm and dry all weekend — into the mid-20s °C with near-zero rain, Saturday the windiest day. Pirelli brought its hardest tyres (C1–C3), pointing to a one-stop race.

## What to watch

- Can **Russell** turn home momentum into a first Silverstone win — and dent Antonelli's lead?
- Does **Red Bull's** Austria form (Verstappen P2) carry to a very different circuit?
- **Ferrari's** upgrades versus their straight-line weakness — and Hamilton's shot at a record-extending 10th home win.
- **Sprint points**: eight are on offer Saturday, enough to quietly reshuffle the top of the table.

Lights out, Sunday 15:00 BST.
